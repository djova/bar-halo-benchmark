# Population result: a narrower scholarly claim

Checked against primary sources on23September2026. This note supplements the
existing Hamilton–Chiba–Ogilvie/Lubow comparison; it does not claim an exhaustive
priority search or change any frozen physical experiment.

[Hamilton et al., sectionsIII.2 andIV.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.03855v2)
explicitly choose an initially linear DF as a local approximation, acknowledge
that detailed modelling needs corrections, and limit extrapolation far from the
resonance. Their stationary boundary treatment also differs from our finite,
positive, unnormalized populations. Therefore, finding that the initial slope
alone can fail is not a new conceptual discovery. Our added evidence is the
quantitative finite-time population contrast and the reusable recorded response
for the stated moving, externally forced model.

[Weinberg & Katz2007](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/375/2/425/1440214)
already emphasize that net bar torque can arise from cancellation among larger
positive and negative orbital transfers. Separating weighted contributions and
tracking their uncertainty is necessary numerical practice, not a new mechanism.
Our gradient-coordinate primitive is also not a literal partition of those
initial orbital cohorts; that distinction must remain explicit.

The already reviewed2026self-consistent studies provide context, not validation
of our imposed operator: [Kodama et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12584) examine
fly-by-driven restoration of halo phase-space gradients, whereas
[Chiba et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01294) examine persistence of stellar
resonance tree rings amid perturbations. Their different populations, forcing
and collective response prevent direct identification with constant additive
slow-action noise in a fixed halo slice.

The useful next claim remains prospective and bounded: can the measured operator
predict an independently evolved new population, and, if a separately measured
cost/precision decision permits, a new3Dhalo-weighted response? The committed
new-population forecasts are being tested; no Cartesian success is claimed.
