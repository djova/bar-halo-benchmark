# Why the hidden pair needs a third integral

The zero-streaming twin construction does not contradict uniqueness results
for two-integral galaxy models. Its DF depends on **E,L²,Lz**, rather than only
E,Lz. The additional orbital variable is essential to the local cancellation.

In the restricted axisymmetric two-integral class, the density fixes the
even-in-Lz component, and the complete intrinsic streaming field fixes the
odd component, under the inversion's assumptions. Magorrian's introduction
and reconstruction explicitly describe this restricted uniqueness and the
higher-moment hierarchy it permits. The same paper explains why allowing a
third integral is a physically different modeling assumption.
[Magorrian (1995), sections1–2](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1995MNRAS.277.1185M).

Hunter & Qian give the contour-inversion method for two-integral models. A
later application to halo velocity distributions also states the one-to-one
relations between density/even DF and streaming/odd DF within that class.
These are established restrictions on what can be inferred after choosing
a two-integral model, not evidence that real halos must obey that restriction.
[Hunter & Qian (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/262.2.401),
[Petač & Ullio (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01531).

Our own perturbation is

\[
\delta F=L_z[g_0(e)+L^2 e^p].
\]

The g0 term and L² term contribute with different powers and angular factors
to the azimuthal first moment. Choosing g0 from the isochrone r²–potential
identity cancels their complete velocity integral at every position. Omitting
the L² dependence would remove the specific compensating contribution used
by this construction. Complete velocity reversal cancels density and all
even moments, while it leaves higher odd moments free to differ.
This paragraph follows from our displayed derivation, not from an inversion
of an observed velocity field.

There is also a distinction between intrinsic moments and a projected
line-of-sight velocity distribution. Work on three-integral orbital recovery
demonstrates why richer kinematic information is relevant to recovering the
internal DF. Our equality of intrinsic even moments and zero local means does
not imply equality of every projected odd moment or of the full velocity
distribution. The current third-moment explorer explicitly exposes one
difference; it is not an observation of dark-matter velocities.
[van de Ven, de Zeeuw & R.C.E. van den Bosch (2008)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.0309).

The scientific inference is therefore conditional: after admitting a smooth
three-integral halo population, unusually strong bulk constraints can still
leave a response difference under an imposed bar. It is not a failure of
two-integral mathematics, an inference that observed halos have this particular
handed structure, or a new discovery that DFs can be nonunique. Stability,
formation history and a live stellar-bar measurement remain additional tests.
