A convenient population can give the wrong torque response. An inexpensive approximation can also work remarkably well. The useful question is how to tell the difference before evolving it.

At identical central density and slope, the selected Gaussian gives the opposite early noise effect from the reference halo. A slope-matched exponential closely agrees with the halo in the tested action slice.

This is a local computational-methods result: one fast-action slice, an externally imposed bar and constant additive action noise. It does not measure a complete halo torque or a physical SIDM effect.

Matching one slope can hide the wrong curvature.

The original experiment held the dynamics fixed and changed the positive population. No profile was renormalized to unit mass. At the later endpoint, the Gaussian’s noise-induced response was 4.735 times the halo-weighted response. The exponential’s point difference was only about 0.068%; that is not a certified sub-0.1% accuracy statement.

The prospective experiment doubled the sweep to s = 0.5, with the same noise η = 0.1, and computed its own response kernel. All profiles, thresholds and numerical forecasts were fixed before independent evolution.

Does the error fit the accuracy budget?

The target uses a conservative lower estimate of the halo response. Numerical refinements are practical error proxies. Near zero, a percentage can be undefined. An unqualified approximation is not necessarily inaccurate.

Where does the allowance come from?

The orange area adds absolute gradient mismatch weighted by the uncertain kernel. The blue signed contribution can cancel between regions. A large orange area can therefore reject an approximation whose actual error is small.

The shaded region selects recorded gradient-coordinate bins, not a cohort of orbits. The full allowance also includes a separately measured kernel-mesh change. Why the primitive kernel measures a gradient ↗

Spend computation on the small response.

The raw estimator averages weighted bar impulses. The cumulative-remainder estimator removes a first-order term whose ensemble integral is known to vanish. It estimates the same observable with a much smaller variance for broad, smooth populations.

What can another researcher use?

The four width-128 rejections are not solely caused by statistical uncertainty. Their absolute-gradient sum, evaluated on the recorded mean kernel, already exceeds the 5% target before its uncertainty is added. Signed contributions cancel; an absolute allowance deliberately discards that cancellation. Inspect a conservative rejection ↗.

Separate the response kernel from the population. Test gradient mismatch across its sensitive region, preserve the physical normalization, and include the kernel’s own uncertainty. A cheap approximation can be useful without being universally safe.

If the full DF and kernel are already available, contracting the full DF directly is cheap. This allowance audits a population approximation; it does not avoid computing the kernel or knowing the reference gradient. The new kernel took about 5,105 worker CPU-seconds, followed by about 0.8 seconds for the full diagnostic. Reuse across populations and the estimator’s variance reduction are distinct sources of efficiency.

The exact gradient inequality, linear dependence on the population and variance reduction through a zero-mean control are established mathematical ideas. The contribution here is a quantified population counterexample, an executable estimator comparison, and an independently testable accuracy procedure in a specified resonance model.

Hamilton and collaborators treated stationary diffusion-supported friction; Chiba anticipated competition with moving-resonance feedback. Ogilvie & Lubow studied a related migration–diffusion problem with different gas physics.
