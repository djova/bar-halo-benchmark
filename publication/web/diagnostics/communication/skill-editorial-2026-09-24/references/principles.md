# Scientific priorities and communication patterns

These are our scientific-communication choices, illustrated by selected primary
papers. The cited passages support particular examples, not an attribution of a
personal style or advice. Coauthorship does not identify who wrote a passage, and
no author's approval of these adaptations is implied. Browser patterns are our own.

## 1. Separate an apparent phenomenon into competing causes

[van den Bosch & Ogiya (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05427), introduction,
sections 4–6, figures 5–12: a visually persuasive disappearance can result from the
physics or the calculation. Idealized experiments vary time step, force accuracy,
softening, particle number and realization separately. Histories and ensembles
expose failures that a single final image or aggregate statistic could conceal.
**Adaptation:** put paired numerical contrasts and their individual signs beside
the visual phenomenon. Their subhalo thresholds are not bar-convergence criteria.

[Chiang, van den Bosch & Schive](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26901), sections 3–7,
figures 1–3 and 10–12, revisits resolution across orbital and anisotropy conditions
and numerical methods. Read later tests with earlier claims; do not freeze a 2018
result as universal doctrine. Here, populations can look stable while individual
objects remain poorly resolved.

## 2. Explain dynamics through phase space, not appearance alone

[Banik & van den Bosch (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05004) and
[Dattathri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11804) connect friction, stalling and
buoyancy to time dependence and orbital/distribution-function structure. Similar
cores need not imply equivalent dynamics. **Adaptation:** show histories, reference
frames and momentum exchange alongside density; distinguish measured transport
from evidence identifying its resonant cause.

[Dattathri et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27480), figures 5–8, links control
experiments, halo response, angular-momentum transfer and a proposed mechanism.
Its gas-free, high-resolution model and restricted parameter domain matter when
interpreting SIDM implications. **Adaptation:** expose the inferential steps rather
than jumping from a visible bar to a dark-matter constraint.

## 3. Model the path from the hidden system to observable data

[van den Bosch, Lange & Zentner (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07547), sections
2–5, builds satellite-kinematic inference around scatter, selection and validation
with increasingly realistic mocks. [Mitra et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03105)
extends this inference. **Adaptation:** make projection and measurement inspectable,
and retain individual catalogue values rather than presenting only a reassuring median.

[Mitra & van den Bosch (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08421) stress-tests model
flexibility. An assumed parameterization can bias even precise inference.
**Adaptation:** keep assumptions next to results and distinguish data uncertainty,
numerical sensitivity and model inadequacy; do not add them into an invented error bar.

## 4. Distinguish individual histories from ensemble statements

[Chiang, van den Bosch & Keim (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26249) compares
identical orbit populations in controlled host shapes, separating population medians,
individual orbital scatter and reconstruction bias. **Adaptation:** pair runs,
show the distribution and avoid interpreting an unchanged median as no effect.

## Explanatory rhythm to borrow, without copying a voice

A recurring useful progression is: concrete scientific puzzle; competing physical
or inferential explanations; an operational definition; a controlled test; a figure
that distinguishes the alternatives; implications limited to the tested conditions.
Figures often expose contrasts in multiple related observables rather than decorate
a prose claim. Translate that into synchronized views and local evidence links.
Keep derivations and exhaustive parameter grids accessible in deeper sections.

The author-search corpus and the actual reading record for the originating Galaxy
Bar application are archived in that project's `research/communication/` directory
and its public `communication.html` page. That record distinguishes broad screening,
full-text section inspection and focused figure review. It does not certify that
every publication in the decade has been discovered or read line by line.

## 5. Ask which reservoir, scale and timescale controls the outcome

The cold-stream and circumgalactic-medium papers widen the scope beyond stellar
orbits. [Mandelker et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05344) organize stream survival
around competition between mixing and cooling; [Aung et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00912)
add the host potential and connect entrainment to galaxy fueling. The
[CGM white paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05644) connects gas reservoirs, recycling,
star formation, metals and kinematics. **Adaptation:** identify where mass, energy,
angular momentum or metals come from and go, and compare the relevant timescales.
A component omitted from a model cannot be represented by decorative animation.

## 6. Make definitions, histories and selection part of the explanation

[van den Bosch (2016 preprint; 2017 publication)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02657),
figure 1, uses a visual taxonomy of evolution channels before counting them. A
classification scheme determines which apparent events become physical claims.
[Villarreal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04327) test how a halo definition changes
assembly-bias measurements. [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13732) separate
physical merger evolution from changing halo-boundary conventions.
**Adaptation:** expose object definitions, apertures and coordinate choices wherever
they can change the result; show historical trajectories rather than imply the
present state uniquely identifies its origin.

[Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04004) control stellar and halo mass when
comparing central and satellite quenching. [Campbell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06347)
test whether one model explains several observables simultaneously.
**Adaptation:** name the matched variables and remaining confounds, and ask what
additional observable would discriminate competing models. A single matched plot
is a weaker result than a mutually consistent explanation of independent data.
