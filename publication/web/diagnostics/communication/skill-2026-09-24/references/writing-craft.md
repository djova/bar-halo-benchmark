# Building an astrophysical argument

This is our synthesis of communication choices in Frank C. van den Bosch and
collaborators' papers, refreshed on 24 September 2026. It is not a reconstruction
of his private intentions, a prescription in his voice, or an endorsement.
First-author work is stronger evidence of his explanatory choices; coauthored
papers show practices of the collaboration. Borrow reasoning and organization,
not distinctive sentences, rhetorical flourishes or unsupported confidence.

The review screened titles and selected abstract passages of 230 arXiv records
from 1994–2026, then revisited body passages in twelve papers, nine first submitted
in 2024–2026. Three recent figure pages were inspected visually. This extends the
earlier 77-paper decade screen. It is not an exhaustive journal bibliography or
a claim that every paper was read in full. The Galaxy Bar communication archive
records the exact coverage, versions, checksums and application.

## Construct the argument before polishing sentences

1. **Name the useful approximation and its possible cost.** Introduce the physical
   quantity that matters, then the assumption normally used to calculate it.
   Explain why it is attractive. State the narrower unanswered question this work
   can resolve. Avoid a generic history of cosmology before reaching the problem.
2. **Make the matched comparison explicit.** Name the quantities held fixed and
   the intervention. If matching the density leaves the DF free, say so. If a
   tracer selection is the physical population rather than a sampling proposal,
   distinguish them before interpreting the result.
3. **Show the hidden variable that explains the result.** An unchanged density,
   median or fit can conceal different orbital content, scatter or model error.
   Identify the information lost by the summary and show how it affects the
   observable. An appealing explanation needs a discriminating measurement.
4. **Give a physical reading of the equation.** Before it: what balance or mapping
   does it express? After it: which term changes, what sign or scale follows, and
   when does the reasoning fail? Keep exact identities, measured approximations,
   and proposed physical mechanisms distinct.
5. **Explain successful simplifications too.** A counterexample establishes
   insufficiency; a successful approximation may identify what must be retained.
   Compare both against the same target and error convention. Do not infer a
   universal criterion from one successful family.
6. **Return a practical consequence.** Conclude with what a researcher can now
   calculate, diagnose or avoid, in which regime, and which missing test controls
   the next extension. State measured outcomes directly; do not substitute audit
   counts, adjectives or speculative applications for a consequence.

## Paragraphs, equations and captions

Use a paragraph to advance one inferential step. A useful sequence is observation
or assumption → evidence → explanation → consequence. Keep subjects concrete:
the distribution weights an orbit; a torque transfers angular momentum; a prior
restricts an inference; softening changes a force. Locate uncertainty in the step
it affects. Delete repeated declarations that the work is rigorous or bounded.

Introduce a symbol with its physical meaning and relevant normalization. Do not
let a dimensionless response inherit the name or units of a full halo torque.
If a sign belongs to a contrast, name both operands. If an error is deterministic
or a refinement proxy, do not depict it as a sampling interval. Use a short
limiting case or worked cancellation when it resolves a likely misunderstanding.
Do not call gradient-coordinate bins literal groups of initial particles.

A principal caption should let a reader reconstruct the comparison: quantity and
units; what changes and what stays fixed; encoding and uncertainty; the visible
result; the inference it supports. Put the interpretive point first when feasible.
Use shared axes and aligned conditions for comparisons. In a browser, a default
selection must already explain something; controls then test another recorded
condition. In a PDF, retain that explanation without requiring the controls.

Keep the main argument continuous. Put complete numeric matrices, implementation
history and operational receipts in appendices or expandable material; retain
limitations that alter the headline beside the result. The conclusion should
answer the introduction's question, not merely repeat the abstract.

## Source-grounded examples

- [van den Bosch & Dattathri, 2511.14912v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14912v3),
  sections 1–2, 4 and figure 12: the introduction moves from the ambiguity of cores
  as evidence to a dynamical test. Before the torque formula, the text explains
  the balance of energy gain and loss through the DF gradient. The figure compares
  DF evolution across hosts and interaction strengths. Adaptation: explain what
  a gradient weights before asking a reader to interpret a response integral.
- [Dattathri et al., 2511.11804v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11804v2), introduction,
  sections 5–9: structurally similar systems motivate the search for a phase-space
  discriminator; the discussion distinguishes proposed explanations and their
  limitations. Adaptation: a density match is a controlled starting point, not a
  guarantee of dynamical equivalence. Do not transfer their self-consistent core
  mechanism into an external-bar calculation.
- [Chiang, van den Bosch & Keim, 2608.26249v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26249v1),
  sections 1–3 and figure 4: the convenience of spherical reconstruction is stated
  before its error is measured. Population medians, individual scatter and inferred
  orbit uncertainty remain separate. Adaptation: identify the precise target of
  an approximation; an accurate aggregate can coexist with inaccurate predictions.
- [Dattathri et al., 2606.27480v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27480v1), section 3.3.2,
  figure 5 and section 5: a switch-off comparison tests an alternative explanation;
  aligned density and bar histories make the intervention legible. Adaptation:
  show the control that distinguishes mechanisms. Their SIDM model and its scope
  are different from additive action noise.
- [Mitra & van den Bosch, 2510.08421v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08421v1),
  sections 5–8, figures 3–4: an ordered set of models is first tested on known
  inputs, then on observations; a shared parameterization can itself impose bias.
  Adaptation: separate successful recovery under assumed rules from adequacy when
  those rules are challenged. Agreement between methods with shared assumptions
  is weaker than agreement with an independently specified reference.
- [Baggen, van den Bosch & Mitra, 2509.01690v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01690v2),
  abstract and section 5: a modest change in measured velocities is traced through
  the mass inference, and a correction is presented with its uncertain controlling
  parameter. Adaptation: report both the immediate measured effect and the bounded
  consequence for inference; explain which uncertain ingredient sets the limit.
- [Chiang, van den Bosch & Schive, 2510.26901v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26901v2),
  sections 3–4, figure 3 and section 7: numerical bias and realization scatter have
  distinct signatures, and older criteria are tested in a broader regime.
  Adaptation: pair a proposed usability criterion with the conditions and failure
  modes it actually covers; never import a numerical threshold by analogy.
- [Chiang, van den Bosch & Schive, 2411.03192v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03192v2),
  methods, figure 3 and section 5: matched density profiles isolate velocity
  anisotropy; orbit geometry explains selective stripping. Adaptation: connect the
  controlled hidden variable to a concrete dynamical pathway, not only a changed
  endpoint.
- [Mitra, van den Bosch & Lange, 2409.03105v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03105v1),
  introduction and conclusion: alternative probes are compared through what they
  measure and which degeneracies remain. Adaptation: motivate an added statistic
  by the information it recovers, not by algorithmic novelty alone.
- [van den Bosch, Lange & Zentner, 1908.07547v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07547v1),
  section 7: assumptions are enumerated and validation tiers deliberately relax
  them. Adaptation: expose the step from verification of a model implementation
  to a test of the model's adequacy; do not merge these evidentiary levels.
- [van den Bosch & Ogiya, 1801.05427v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05427v1),
  section 4, figures 4–5 and discussion: numerical interventions answer different
  questions from physical ones. Adaptation: include enough numerical evidence to
  assess the effect without making the audit trail the scientific narrative.
- [van den Bosch, 1611.02657v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02657v1), summary and
  evolution-channel definitions: operational categories determine what apparent
  changes mean. Adaptation: define the measured event, contrast or classification
  before attaching a physical interpretation to it.

## Final editorial test

Read the abstract, first result figure and conclusion without the methods. Can
the reader state the question, controlled change, measured answer, explanation and
domain? Then read the methods alone: can the reader recover the observable,
normalization, sampling unit, controls and failure criteria? Finally check that
both readings make the same claim. If they do not, revise the claim before the prose.
