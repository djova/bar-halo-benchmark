## The bounded claim to scrutinize

At matched central density and slope, positive populations can give opposite
finite-time noise responses in one prescribed moving-resonance model. The
recorded kernel separates this population effect from the orbital evolution.
A tested gradient-mismatch diagnostic is intended to identify useful cheap
approximations, with explicit numerical and sampling qualifications. The
cumulative-remainder estimator makes the calculation cheaper for some weights;
its measured limits are part of the result. No full-halo or physical SIDM claim.

## Questions about priority and interpretation

1. Is there a published, mathematically equivalent population-selection
   counterexample in the stationary or moving-resonance literature that should
   be credited more directly? We already recognize that the local-linear
   approximation, competing diffusion mechanisms and linear response to an
   externally evolved initial population are established ideas.
2. Does the cumulative identity reduce to an existing control-variate, δf, or
   integration-by-parts estimator under the same canonical-map and additive-
   noise assumptions? Which primary reference is closest? We have not
   established priority for this construction.
3. Are reference-halo weighting at fixed fast actions and the associated
   canonical measure implemented consistently with how you would define a
   conditional resonance contribution? Is any implicit selection still being
   mistaken for a complete halo response?

## Questions about usefulness and the next physical test

4. Would the kernel-weighted gradient allowance help you choose between a
   cheap exponential/local expansion and a more detailed DF? Are the cost,
   conservatism and assumptions clear enough to use it in another code?
5. Is the simultaneous Monte Carlo uncertainty treatment adequate for a
   computational-methods note, given that its Student-t and numerical-
   refinement allowances are operational rather than rigorous bounds?
6. Which missing physical ingredient would make the most discriminating next
   test: varying fast actions, coefficient variation across the active region,
   temporally correlated forcing, overlapping resonances, or collective halo
   response? We would prefer one falsifiable test to a broader parameter survey.
7. Does the note clearly separate the new quantitative benchmark from the
   mechanisms anticipated by Hamilton et al., Chiba, and Ogilvie & Lubow?
8. In what actual workflow would an approximation allowance be more useful
   than directly contracting an available full DF with the same kernel? The
   current criterion is an audit of population representation; it does not
   save the cost of generating a kernel or avoid knowledge of the reference
   gradient. Is a compact closure, a reusable family or an independently known
   gradient envelope the most useful application?
